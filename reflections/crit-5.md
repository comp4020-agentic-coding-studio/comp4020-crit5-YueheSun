# Crit 5 reflection

**What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?**

The real breakthrough wasn't a piece of code, it was realizing I had to
stay in the loop at every step, not just check in at the end. Early on,
before there was even a UI, I sat there and literally listened to the
music while we worked out where the turning points should go. When I
let the AI tune the frequency of turns on its own, it always got
something subtly wrong — too many turns packed together, or an opening
that felt overwhelming for a new player. But once I listened by ear and
told it exactly what felt off, it fixed the actual problem instead of
guessing again. Checking after every small change also meant we only
ever solved one problem at a time, instead of the AI juggling several
loosely related issues at once and getting worse at all of them.

I also noticed a pattern: whenever its answers started drifting away
from what I actually asked, that usually meant its attention had gotten
spread too thin over a long conversation — that was my cue to clear
things and start fresh. A lot of this project was also spent nudging
mechanic details by hand, bit by bit, rather than writing one long,
fully-specified prompt up front and letting it build everything at once.
Both ways of working have real trade-offs between speed and control.

**What did this change about who I want to be as a developer?**

Mostly, how much I trust an AI's own word. If it says a bug is fixed,
that's a claim, not a fact — I want to be the kind of developer who
always checks it myself before believing it.
